Side effects of lorazepam

Lorazepam can be helpful when prescribed correctly, but it may cause side effects, especially if taken at the wrong dose or mixed with alcohol, opioids, sleeping tablets, or other sedatives. If side effects feel severe, unusual, or long-lasting, speak with a doctor or pharmacist as soon as possible.

  • Daytime drowsiness, sleepiness, tiredness, or reduced concentration.
  • Dizziness, light-headedness, weakness, or unsteady movement.
  • Confusion, forgetfulness, low mood, or changes in behavior.
  • Nausea, headache, blurred vision, or reduced coordination.
  • Serious warning signs may include breathing difficulty, extreme sleepiness, fainting, allergic reaction, or unusual agitation.

FAQ about Lorazepam (Ativan 2mg)

What is lorazepam (Ativan 2 mg) used for?

It is prescribed to treat acute stress, anxiety, panic attacks, and sometimes short-term insomnia caused by anxiety.

How fast does lorazepam (Ativan 2 mg) work?

Usually, it takes between thirty and sixty minutes to get going. Because of this, it is not used for long-term daily therapy but rather for temporary relief.

What should I be careful about?

Common side effects include exhaustion, dizziness, and trouble focusing. Because long-term use of alcohol can result in dependence, it is strictly prohibited.

Can I take lorazepam (Ativan 2 mg) every day?

Lorazepam is generally intended for short-term use. Taking it daily for an extended period should only be done under a doctor's supervision because it can lead to tolerance and dependence.

Can I drive after taking lorazepam (Ativan 2 mg)?

No. Lorazepam may cause drowsiness, slowed reaction times, and impaired coordination. Avoid driving or operating machinery until you know how the medication affects you.

Can I stop taking lorazepam (Ativan 2 mg) suddenly?

No. Stopping lorazepam abruptly after regular use may cause withdrawal symptoms such as anxiety, insomnia, irritability, or, in severe cases, seizures. Your healthcare provider may recommend gradually reducing the dose.
